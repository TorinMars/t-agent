# Learn English: review with FSRS

> Part of the T-Agent design document. The index of all parts is [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

The spaced review of the Learn cards ([learn-english.md](learn-english.md)) schedules every card with FSRS-6, the Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler, since schema 22. Before it, Learn used an SM-2-like ladder (again 10 minutes, hard × 1.2, good × ease through the steps 1, 3, 7, 15, 30 days, at most 365).

- **Code**: `services/learn-fsrs.js` (the FSRS-6 formulas and weights, pure), `services/learn-review.js` (the grades, the relearning step, intervals, the replay, the preview, the retrievability, `isKnown`; pure), `services/learn-schedule.js` (the replay of stored cards, the reschedule, the schema 22 migration; database helpers), `services/learn.js` (answers, the API fields, the setting), `services/learn-sync.js` (the replay after a merge).
- **Pace** (schema 23): "New words a day" and "Keeps slipping" below; `services/learn.js` (the queues, `pace`, `addNewWords`, `slips`, the parts and tips), `test/learn-pace.test.js` (the cap in spell, review and build, `extra=practice`, first answers, `/new-words` and the next day, the summary, the leech rule, the parts and tips with a fake chat model, the schema 23 columns, a wrong hard answer), the engines in `test/learn-sync.test.js` (the setting and the words added today merged, the parts and the tip, a company computer), the 22 → 23 start in `test/data-home.test.js`.
- **Tests**: `test/learn-fsrs.test.js` (the formulas against the reference test vectors), `test/learn-review.test.js` (grades, relearning, same day, cap, retention, replay, estimate, preview, retrievability, known words), `test/learn-schedule.test.js` (the API fields, an answer without a state, the desired retention through the route, the 21 → 22 replay on a fixture database, two databases that answered apart, an older peer, a company computer), the 21 → 22 start in `test/data-home.test.js`.

### Review rules

- **Model and weights.** FSRS-6 with its 21 default weights, exactly as the reference implementation ts-fsrs v5.4.2 ("v5.4.2 using FSRS-6.0", `packages/fsrs/src/algorithm.ts` and `src/constant.ts`; the same model as the fsrs4anki wiki "The Algorithm" and fsrs-rs): `0.212, 1.2931, 2.3065, 8.2956, 6.4133, 0.8334, 3.0194, 0.001, 1.8722, 0.1666, 0.796, 1.4835, 0.0614, 0.2629, 1.6483, 0.6014, 1.8729, 0.5425, 0.0912, 0.0658, 0.1542`. No new dependency: the formulas are written out in `services/learn-fsrs.js`, rounding every intermediate value to 8 decimals where ts-fsrs does, so the results equal the reference to the last digit. The weights are not trained on the person's answers (not yet). No fuzz: the same answers always give the same schedule.
- **Formulas** (days are real numbers, ratings G 1 to 4):
  - forgetting curve `R(t, S) = (1 + factor · t / S)^decay`, `decay = −w20`, `factor = 0.9^(1/decay) − 1`, so `R(S, S) = 0.9`;
  - interval `I(r, S) = S / factor · (r^(1/decay) − 1)` at the desired retention r, rounded to whole days, at least 1, at most 365 (`MAX_INTERVAL`);
  - first answer `S0(G) = w[G−1]`, `D0(G) = w4 − e^(w5·(G−1)) + 1` clamped to [1, 10];
  - difficulty `D' = w7 · D0(4) + (1 − w7) · (D + ΔD · (10 − D) / 9)` with `ΔD = −w6 · (G − 3)` (linear damping and mean reversion), clamped to [1, 10];
  - recall (hard, good) `S' = S · (1 + e^w8 · (11 − D) · S^−w9 · (e^(w10·(1−R)) − 1) · w15 (hard only))`;
  - forgetting (again) `S' = w11 · D^−w12 · ((S + 1)^w13 − 1) · e^(w14·(1−R))`, at most `S / e^(w17·w18)` (the FSRS-6 cap);
  - same day (less than one day since the previous answer) `S' = S · S^−w19 · e^(w17·(G − 3 + w18))`, never below S for hard or good.
- **Grades.** The app has three grades: again, hard and good are the FSRS ratings 1, 2 and 3 (there is no Easy). The grade alone decides the schedule; `correct` is kept for the mistakes (`last_correct`). The page sends `grade: 'hard'` with `correct: false` for a word that is one letter off ("Almost"): FSRS schedules it as hard (whole days, not the 10 minutes of again), it is not a lapse (`lapses` counts again only), it counts as missed for "Keeps slipping", and it stays in Mistakes (`last_correct` 0).
- **A new card** (never answered) is due at once. Its first answer sets S and D from `S0` and `D0`: with the default weights again gives 10 minutes, hard 1 day (S 1.29), good 2 days (S 2.31). Starter words still become due 10 a day (`STARTER_PER_DAY`).
- **again** keeps a relearning step: the card is due again in 10 minutes (`AGAIN_MS`, `interval_days` 0) with the stability after forgetting, and `lapses` + 1. The answer after it is a same-day review and schedules the card by FSRS again.
- **hard / good**: the interval of the new stability at the desired retention; `due_at` = the answer time + that many days. There is no "good after hard" ordering between the grades (ts-fsrs keeps good at least one day after hard): the interval depends on the stability alone, so a replay and a reschedule give the same date. After a lapse, hard and good can therefore give the same number of days.
- **Elapsed time.** `t` is whole 24-hour days since the previous answer (`floor`), and less than one day is the same-day review. ts-fsrs counts UTC calendar days; whole days are the same on every engine whatever its time zone (the sync needs the same answers to give the same state everywhere).
- **Every answer** adds one to `reps` and sets `last_result`, `last_at` and `last_correct`, and stores the new `fsrs_stability` and `fsrs_difficulty`.
- **ease** is no longer used for the schedule. It stays in the table and in the sync because older versions read it; it is kept unchanged (2.5 for a new card, what it was for an older one).
- **A card without an FSRS state that was answered before** (its state came from an older version): the answer builds on the replay of its answers when they are all here (below); otherwise (a company computer's cache, answers still on their way) on an estimate from the old state: stability = `interval_days` (at retention 0.9 the interval is the stability), or the first "again" stability when the interval is 0; difficulty from the ease, 2.5 → `D0(good)`, 1.3 → 10.
- **Known words** (`isKnown`, used by picking words from translations): right at least twice with the last answer right, or a stability of 21 days or more when the card has an FSRS state (before: a review interval of 21 days or more, which is still the rule for a card without one).
- **Mistake**, **days**, streak and done today are unchanged (see "Review rules" in [learn-english.md](learn-english.md)); new today is the cards first answered today ("New words a day" below).

### Desired retention

- The Learn setting `desired_retention` in `learn_v1:<owner>` (`SCHEDULE_SETTINGS` in `services/learn.js`): 0.85, 0.9 or 0.95, default 0.9. A higher value gives shorter intervals and more reviews. `PUT /api/learn/settings { desired_retention }` checks it (400 "The desired retention must be 0.85, 0.9 or 0.95").
- Changing it reschedules, in the same transaction as the saved settings: every card of the owner with an FSRS state and a last answer gets `due_at` = its last answer + the interval of its stability at the new retention (`services/learn-schedule.js` `reschedule`); a card whose last answer was again keeps its 10 minutes; new cards are not touched. The stability and difficulty do not depend on the retention, only the dates do.
- It travels with the other learning settings in the Learn sync (the later version wins). An engine that receives a new value reschedules its cards the same way in the transaction of that page, with no clock and no change number (the same stability gives the same date on every engine). An older peer does not know the setting and sends settings without it: the value here is kept, not reset to the default.

### New words a day

Up to v2.154.0 the spell queue gave the due cards and then every never-practised card with no cap (the starter list spread its due dates over the days, but spell ignored them): one day had 141 new words. Now every queue gives new cards up to a daily count.

- **Setting** `new_per_day` in `learn_v1:<owner>` (`PACE_SETTINGS` in `services/learn.js`): 5, 10, 15, 20 or 30, default 10; `PUT /api/learn/settings { new_per_day }` (another value: 400 "Invalid learning settings"). It travels with the learning settings; an older peer's settings without it keep the value here (`KEPT_SETTINGS`, like `desired_retention`).
- **A new card** is a visible word card with `reps` = 0. **New today** (`new_today`) is the number of cards whose first answer (the earliest `learn_reviews` row of the card) falls on today, the calendar day of `done_today` (the server's time zone). A card with fewer answers here than its `reps` is left out (its first answer is not here). So the count is the same on every linked engine once the answers have synced.
- **Allowance**: `new_per_day` + the words added today; `new_left = max(0, allowance − new_today)`.
- **Queues** (`GET /api/learn/queue`, every mode but `mistakes`), in this order, at most `limit` cards: (a) the due cards that are not new (`reps > 0 AND due_at <= now`), oldest due first; (b) new cards, at most `new_left`, by `due_at`, then `id` (the starter list's words of today before those of the next days); in `review` only new cards due now, in the other modes any new word that fits the mode. Fill and the retained build mode require an example; (c) only with `extra=practice` (another value adds nothing): the learnt cards that are not due, the least recently practised first (the order up to v2.154.0). The page's Practice asks for one spelling queue with `extra=practice`. Practise anyway uses the current mode's word queue with the same flag. Spell still reads twice the cards and leaves out a word without a meaning while the translator fails; a word left out does not use the allowance. `mistakes` is unchanged (no cap). Each queue card has `is_new`.
- **"5 more new words"**: `POST /api/learn/new-words { count }` (an integer 1–50, else 400 "Invalid learning settings") raises today's allowance by `count` and answers `{ new_per_day, new_today, new_left }`. It is kept in the learning settings as `extra_new: { day: 'YYYY-MM-DD', count }` (the server's day; at most 500), so the settings sync carries it, and it counts only on that day (the next day the allowance is `new_per_day` again). `PUT /settings` takes the field and keeps the stored value (a page that sends the settings back is not refused). Merge: `extra_new` does not follow the last writer; the later day wins, on the same day the larger count (`mergeExtraNew`); an engine whose newer settings lose a peer's larger count takes it with a new version, so it reaches every engine. Two taps at the same moment on two engines that had not synced yet count once. A company computer makes it on its home (`POST /v1/learn-sync/new-words`, 503 while the home cannot be reached).
- **Summary** (`GET /api/learn/summary`): `new_per_day`, `new_today`, `new_left`; `due` = the due cards that are not new + `min(new_left, the new cards due now)`, the number Review gives; `minutes_today`: today's answers by time, the gaps between consecutive answers each counted up to 120 s, plus 15 s for the first answer of each run (a run starts after a gap longer than 120 s), in whole minutes (`studyMinutes`). The page uses it for the note to take a break.
- **Company computer.** Its summary is the home's. Its queues count the first answers it still keeps, or the home's `new_today` of the last word-only summary copy (`learn_home_v1`) from today when that is larger: after its answers are handed over, the count follows the home's summary, which the page reads with every home screen.
- The background translation ahead of time (`prefill`) takes the word cards in the same order (due, new by due date, the rest).

### Keeps slipping

A word that is missed again and again is learned in parts on the page (the board "Learn at a calmer pace": copy it with its parts, then type it from memory).

- **Leech rule** (`slips` in `services/learn.js`): `missed` = the wrong answers of the card (`correct = 0`, a wrong hard answer included); `leech` = `missed >= 4` and not (its last two answers were both right and on different days, the calendar days of `done_today`). Two right answers on the same day do not end it; a new wrong answer starts it again. Every queue card (the `mistakes` mode too) has `missed` and `leech`. On a company computer, whose answers leave once its home has them, `missed` is at least the card's `lapses`, and the exit looks at the answers still there.
- **Parts and tip.** Every queue card has `parts` (the word in syllables, `["tem","po","rar","i","ly"]`, or `null`) and `tip` (one short line of plain English, or `''`). For a leech word card without both, the chat model of the translator (`services/translator.js` `chat`, JSON, temperature 0, 30 s) is asked once per word while the server runs: `SPELLING_SYSTEM` asks for the syllables as written and one true sentence, never an invented rule, an empty tip when there is none; only the English word is sent. Checks: the parts joined must give the word exactly (case and hyphens aside; letters, hyphens and apostrophes; at most 12), else no parts; the tip one line of at most 200 characters with no Chinese, else none. They are stored in `learn_cards.parts` (joined with a middle dot, U+00B7) and `tip` (schema 23, `''` until known).
- **When.** Like the meanings: the queue waits at most 2.5 s for its first two cards (`QUEUE_HEAD`), the others are filled in the background after the answer (`settle()` for tests), one at a time. A failure of the model (none set up, the service away) pauses the asks for 10 minutes; the queue never waits for it and the cards come without parts. A word whose answer had nothing usable is not asked again while the server runs. The service the routes use (`sharedLearn`) has this server's chat model; other services (the sync's, tests by default) ask nothing.
- **Sync.** `parts` and `tip` are content like `phonetic`: they travel with the card in the Learn sync, a filled-in value keeps the content clock and never loses to an empty one, a moved card's never come over, a peer's are checked again (parts that do not give the word are dropped), and an older peer (schema 22) sends none. Changing the English of a card clears them.

### Replay, sync and migration

- **The state of a card is the replay of its answers**: its `learn_reviews`, ordered by `at` (a tie by `uid`, the same on every engine), each grade as its rating, from a new card, with these weights and the owner's desired retention (`review.replay`, `learn-schedule.js` `replayed`). It gives every review-state field: `due_at`, `interval_days`, `reps` (the number of answers), `lapses` (the agains), `last_*`, `fsrs_stability`, `fsrs_difficulty`, with `ease` kept. Same answers → same state.
- **Complete history.** A card is replayed only when it has at least one answer and at least as many answers as `reps`. A company computer's cache does not (its answers leave once its home has them), nor a card whose state arrived before its answers; such a card keeps the state it has, so no progress is lost. A card with no answer stays new.
- **A new answer** updates from the stored state (the same as a replay with one more answer), or replays first when the state is NULL.
- **Sync** (`services/learn-sync.js`, the half "review state" of a card in [learn-english.md](learn-english.md) "Sync across linked devices"): the FSRS state travels with the review state (`fsrs_stability`, `fsrs_difficulty` in the card; checked to [0.001, 36500] and [1, 10], else NULL), and answers stay a union by uid. After a page is applied, every card whose review state or answers changed there is replayed when its history is complete (in the page's transaction, with a new change number when the replay changed it). So two engines that answered the same card while apart end with the same state after the merge, whichever way the pages go: the replay of the union of the answers. The review-state clock of the replay stays the merged one, so nothing goes back and forth (the peer replays the same answers to the same state).
- **An older peer** (schema 21 or older) does not know the FSRS columns: its card arrives with NULL, its own answers follow the old rules, and it keeps reading and writing `interval_days`, `ease` and `due_at`. When the state that wins here has NULL with `reps` > 0 and the replay gives another schedule (the fields an older version reads), the replay also gets a new review-state clock of this engine, so the older peer takes the replayed schedule in the next round; its own next answer is again recomputed here once that answer is here. A replay that only adds the FSRS state to a schedule that came through an older peer (which drops the state) keeps the clock, so nothing circles through it. The pull kind (`full-21`) is unchanged: the state is derived, so links need not read each other again.
- **A company computer** applies its home's cards as they come (with their FSRS state) and replays nothing; its own answer builds on that stored state, and the home replays the card when the answer arrives.
- **Migration 21 → 22** (`services/learn-sync-store.js` `ensureSyncSchema`, once, when it adds the two columns, inside its transaction; `db/index.js` copies the database to the backups folder first as for every older generation and logs the counts): every card with a complete history gets the state of its replay (also `interval_days` and `due_at`: when the replayed due date differs from the stored one, the replayed one is taken, earlier or later). A card whose state changed gets the review-state clock `max(now, newest stored + 1)` of this engine and a change number after every stored one (those of the settings and the level in `system_state` too), so the linked engines that do not compute it (an older version, a company computer) take the replayed schedule; an engine of this version replays the same answers to the same state. Cards without answers and cards whose answers are not all here are left as they are, with NULL.
- **Migration 22 → 23** adds `learn_cards.parts` and `tip` (`TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT ''`) the same way; nothing is computed.
- **Known limits.** The FSRS weights are the defaults for everyone (no optimizer yet). While some engines are older, they schedule their own answers with the old rules until this engine replays them. A tie of two answers in the same millisecond is ordered by uid, not by who answered first.
