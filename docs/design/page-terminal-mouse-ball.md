# The page: mouse ball of the terminal

> Part of the T-Agent design document. The index of all parts is [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

## Mouse ball

Why (the user, 2026-11-26): copying from the phone terminal felt unusable; the double / triple tap selects only a word or a line and the copy view is a separate screen. The mouse ball works like a mouse, after the "fv floating ball" app. Mockup confirmed by the user (seven boards: idle, drag, press, select, copied, menu, reaching the far right). 2026-11-27 (the user: "make the floating mouse blend into the floating scrollbar on the right; when the floating scrollbar slides left, it turns into the mouse"; mockup confirmed: idle, slide left, detached, press, select, copied, first-time hint, far right): there is no separate ball and no switch any more; the wheel strip is the entrance. 2026-11-27 (the user, mockup confirmed: idle, handle, move, left): a separate mouse handle is a second entrance; see "The mouse handle" below. Later on 2026-11-27 (the user, mockup confirmed: "the scrollbar is gone, the mouse handle on the right edge does both jobs"): the wheel strip is removed and the handle is the only entrance; it also scrolls ("Scrolling" below).

- **Where and when.** Wherever a terminal is shown, in both input modes (the user, 2026-11-27: "PC and mobile both have the same"), out of the mouse handle ("The mouse handle" below), in the normal and the alternate buffer, with or without history. With a mouse it is the same gesture with the left button held: press the handle, slide it toward the inside, the ball at 40px, keep still 600 ms to press, move to select, the mouseup copies (the user activation of the mouseup), and the ball glides back; the cursor is 46px up-left of the mouse pointer as of the finger. Escape cancels it like a cancelled touch (nothing copied, the selection cleared, a program gets its release; the key is not sent to the terminal: a capture `keydown` listener while the ball is out). On a computer the More-menu switch below is not shown (it is in the phone's More menu): the ball always selects in the page there, and a real mouse already reaches a program directly. Nothing to turn on (the More menu switch `#h5-mouse-ball-switch` and the per-device `h5-mouse-ball` key of v2.174 are gone; a stored old value is ignored); the only switch decides who gets the button (below).
- **Code.** `scripts/h5-shell/MouseBall.jsx` (drawing, the mouse handle and its touches: the scroll, the pull-out and the move; the finger after the pull-out, the glide, the hint), `scripts/h5-shell/mouse-ball-model.mjs` (pure: the handle's axis, the stretch, the glide target, the geometry, the dwell reducer, the handle's place, the scroll's pull and speed), the chunk `screen-mouse-ball.jsx` registered in `lazy-screens.jsx` as `MouseBallScreen` (loaded in both input modes, after the first screen; drawn by `App.jsx` with `BALL_KEYS`). The first screen carries only the lazy registration and the icons; nothing of the handle or its scrolling is in `h5.js` or the first screen. `h5.js` `ui.actions.mouseBallTarget()` hands the chunk the shown terminal: `{ term, el, screen, copy, hookSelection, gesture }`, or null while there is none (empty, a hidden or disposed session, the agent chat over it, the copy view open). The chunk is also not live while the terminal area is empty, a notebook, the mind maps or the Learn page are shown. It looks every 500 ms whether the terminal of the touch is still there (and ends the touch when not) and whether the hint is due.
- **Idle.** Only the mouse handle: no ball, no scrollbar of the page's own (xterm's scrollbar is the position). The handle is the ball at rest.
- **Out of the handle** (the mockup's "Slide the handle left"). A one-finger touch on the handle that travels more sideways than up or down and toward the inside in its first 10px (`handleAxis`) is the ball; up or down is a scroll and never becomes the mouse, toward the edge is nothing. Then, while the finger is less than 40px from the touch toward the inside (`DETACH`, `stretchProgress` 0 to 1), the handle stretches toward the finger like a bubble (`stretchShape`): a head under the finger (never outside the handle's edge) that grows from the bar's 11px to the 52px ball (a `scale` of the ball element, opacity 0.4 to 0.95), and a tail (white 30%, round caps) from the bar (`barAnchor`: the bar's x at the touch's height kept inside the bar) to the head, as wide as the head up to 20px. The cursor and the dashed line already show where the cursor will be, fainter the less it is stretched. The ball has `data-state="stretch"` and `data-progress`; the handle has `data-mouse="stretch"` and its bar is blue (primary 90%). Lifted (or cancelled) before 40px it springs back into the handle (the glide below) and nothing happens: no click, no scroll, no copy, nothing sent to a program. At 40px it lets go: the ball, centred under the finger (no grab offset), `data-state="drag"`, the handle `data-mouse="ball"`, and the dwell starts.
- **The ball.** A 52px round ball (blue grey 800, a 1px light edge, elevation 6, opacity 0.95, the `mouseBall` icon at 22px), `position: fixed` above the terminal (z-index `speedDial`, under menus, dialogs and the notice), `pointer-events: none` (the handle has the finger). It follows the finger (`ballFor`), its centre inside the visible terminal (`#h5-terminal` within the visual viewport, so above the on-screen keyboard). The cursor is the top-left corner of a square: two lines of 19px (2.5px white over a 5px black outline, butt caps, a mitred corner) meeting at the corner, which is the mouse position. It is drawn 46px left of and 46px above the ball's centre, with a thin dashed line (white 45%, 1.5px, 3 4) between them, in one full-viewport SVG (`pointer-events: none`, `aria-hidden`).
- **Edge rule** (user-approved, "Reaching the far right"): the finger cannot pass the screen edge, so a cursor 46px to the left would stop 46px short of the last column. The sideways offset shrinks linearly to 0 over the last 80px before the right edge of the visible terminal and the upward offset over the last 80px above its bottom edge (`cursorOffset`); at the edge the cursor is straight above (or at) the ball and reaches the last column and the last row, in between it glides without a jump. The grab offset fades the same way, relative to how much of the fade there was at the touch (`ballFor`), so a ball grabbed on its right side would still reach the edge (out of the handle the ball is under the finger, so there is no grab to fade). The cursor is kept inside the visible cells (`cursorFor`, the screen's box within the visible terminal).
- **Dwell = button down.** With the finger down, a ball that moved less than 6px for 600 ms presses the left button at the cursor (`dwellStep`, a pure reducer over `down` / `move` / `tick` / `up` / `cancel` with the event times; a move of more than 6px restarts the wait from there; a move that comes after the wait was over presses first, where the ball stood). While waiting, a ring (an SVG circle just outside the ball, the primary colour, 3px) fills over the 600 ms; with `prefers-reduced-motion` it does not fill and shows whole once pressed. On the press: a haptic tick (Osk.jsx `keyTick`, the strength of Settings > Keyboard for this device, nothing when it is Off), the ball turns `notice` blue, the cursor turns the primary blue (`#90caf9`), `aria-pressed` is true.
- **Status pill** (v2.176.1, after the report "the mouse appears, but after the pause, dragging selects no text"). While the ball is out, a pill at the top of the terminal (`#h5-mouse-ball-status`, `data-status`) says what it is doing: "Hold still to press" while the dwell clock runs, then "Selecting in the page" (no mouse reporting: xterm's selection and the copy of this page) or "Mouse sent to the program" (the program has mouse reporting on: it selects and copies by itself). It never takes a touch and goes with the lift. It tells a person, and whoever debugs a report, whether the press happened at all and who got the button.
- **Who gets the button** (v2.176.4, after the report that the pill said "Mouse sent to the program" and nothing was selected in Claude Code). **Since v2.177.2 the default is the program** (the user: "default on, add the switch on the computer too", after the byte capture showed the haptic tick's focus pair and v2.177.1 made Claude Code select): a program with mouse reporting on gets the mouse, any other session (a shell, `less`, a Claude without full screen) is selected in the page automatically (`buttonMode`), and the key `off` makes the page select always. What follows describes v2.176.4, when the default was the page: the ball selected with xterm's selection and copied through the page's copy path, whether or not the program had mouse reporting on (Claude Code did not select with the synthetic mouse the ball sent, and the page's selection reads the screen it shows, the alternate screen of a full-screen program too). Sending the hover, the press, the motion and the release to the program ("program mode", below) ran only when this device's switch was on (the key `h5-mouse-ball-program` = `on`; since v2.177.2 it runs unless the key is `off`; 2026-11-27, the user: "add the hover events and a switch"); with it off the pill says "Selecting in the page" and nothing goes to the program.
- **The switch** (2026-11-27). "Mouse ball sends the mouse to programs" in the header's More menu on the phone and the computer (`#h5-mouse-ball-program` in `Header.jsx` `MoreMenu`, after "Terminal width" on the phone), shown while a terminal session is shown (`remember`; where the handle and so the ball can be), on by default (since v2.177.2). A menu row (`menuitemcheckbox` with `aria-checked`, 44px, the `mouseBall` icon) with the helper line "Off: the page selects. On: a program with mouse reporting, such as Claude Code in full screen, selects by itself." and a drawn MD2 switch at the end (one span: MUI's `Switch` is not in the first screen). A tap flips it and the row shows the new state at once; the menu stays open. It is per device (category hardware in `public/js/local-keys.js`: a choice for this touch screen, not synced): `off` or removed (= on), through `local-keys.mjs`; the menu reads it when it opens, the ball at the detach (for the hover) and at every press. The status pill keeps telling which mode a press used.
- **Two modes** (2026-11-27, the user: "Change the floating ball selection"). Who gets the button is decided once, at the press (`buttonMode` in `mouse-ball-model.mjs`, from xterm's `term.modes.mouseTrackingMode`), and kept until the lift or cancel, even when the program changes its mouse mode in the middle of the gesture (the events it no longer asks for are then dropped by xterm):
  - **page** (`none`: a plain shell, Claude Code without its fullscreen mode, Codex): the page selects and copies (Select, Scrolling while selecting, Lift and Copied text below).
  - **program** (`x10`, `vt200`, `drag`, `any`: Claude Code in fullscreen, vim, tmux, opencode; with the switch on): the ball is the program's mouse. Programs such as these select by themselves (Claude Code knows where it wrapped its lines, a double click selects a word or an address, a triple click a line) and copy on the release through OSC 52 or pbcopy, which arrive through `terminal-clipboard.js` like any program copy. The ball dispatches real (untrusted) `mousedown` / `mousemove` / `mouseup` events with `button` 0 and `buttons` 1 while held at the cursor, and `WheelEvent`s (`deltaMode` line, one per line) for the edge scroll, on xterm's `.xterm-screen`; xterm turns them into the reports of the program's tracking mode and encoding (X10, SGR 1006, ...) for the cell under the cursor (1-based screen cells, not buffer rows) and sends them through `term.onData` like a real mouse (`send` in `h5.js` does not move the wide view for a mouse report). What is sent follows the mode (`programReports`): the press always; the release not in x10; the motion with the button held in drag and any (xterm reports one per changed cell); wheel steps not in x10 (in x10 xterm would turn a wheel event into arrow keys or its own scroll). **Hover and focus, as a real mouse** (2026-11-27, after a byte comparison of a real mouse drag with the ball in this page, modes 1000/1002/1003/1004/1006 on: the press `ESC[<0;9;4M`, the drags `ESC[<32;..M` and the release `ESC[<0;15;5m` were the same, but before the press the real mouse sent a hover `ESC[<35;9;4M` and, as its click focused the terminal, a focus-in `ESC[I`, and the ball neither; Claude Code (Ink) may need the pointer position or the focus before it starts a selection). With the switch on and mouse reporting on at the detach (`drag.hover`), the ball dispatches `mousemove` with `button` 0 and `buttons` 0 at the cursor whenever it moves while the ball is out and the button is not down; xterm reports it only in any-event tracking (mode 1003, `programReports(..).hover`, checked at each move) and only once per cell (`ESC[<35;c;rM` in SGR); in the other modes nothing is sent, as with a real mouse. At the press: the terminal takes the focus first when its textarea is not the active element (`term.focus()`; xterm sends `ESC[I` when the program asked for focus reports, mode 1004), then a hover at the cell of the press (dropped by xterm when it was the last one), then the press. After the release nothing more is sent but what xterm sends itself (a lift without a move still sends one motion at the cell of the press before the release, as before). **The haptic tick comes before the focus** (2026-11-27, a `cat -v` capture on a phone showed `ESC[O` `ESC[I` between the press and the first drag, which a real mouse never sends): on iOS the tick toggles a hidden switch that takes the focus from the terminal and gives it back, so `keyTick()` is called before `term.focus()` and the press, not after. Nothing of this in page mode or with the switch off. No pointer, touch or click events are made, so the page's tap chain, copy view and copy-on-select do not run, and `terminal-clipboard.js` counts only trusted events anyway. xterm focuses its textarea on the `mousedown` as for any click (it has `inputmode="none"`, so no system keyboard). An old page highlight is cleared at the press; no page highlight is drawn (the program draws its own).
  - How a person tells them apart: the ball, the ring and the blue cursor look the same in both; in program mode the highlight is the program's own (Claude Code's colours, not xterm's) and nothing is copied before the program says so; the ball carries `data-mode` (`page` / `program`) while pressed. Claude Code turns mouse reporting on only in its fullscreen mode (`/tui fullscreen` or `CLAUDE_CODE_NO_FLICKER=1`, the default for new users); without it the ball selects in the page.
- **Select** (page mode). With the button down the text from the press point to the cursor is selected with xterm's own selection (`term.select(col, row, length)`; xterm draws the highlight in the DOM renderer). Both ends are cells of the buffer (`cellFromPoint`: the column boundary nearest to the point, as a mouse selection counts it, and the screen row plus `buffer.active.viewportY`), so one start and one length cover several rows, wrapped or not (`selectionRange`); `getSelection` joins wrapped rows and ends real lines with a newline. The xterm screen's bounding box includes the 4px inset, the sideways offset of Wide mode and the keyboard shift. New output clears the highlight as after a double tap (`hookSelection`); the ball draws it again at once while the button is down.
- **Scrolling while selecting.** With the button down and the cursor within 24px of the top or bottom edge of the visible cells, the terminal scrolls by lines (`term.scrollLines`, every frame, counted by real time): 4 lines per second at the inner edge of the zone up to 40 at the edge (`scrollSpeed`); it stops when the cursor leaves the zone or the finger is lifted. The press point stays on its buffer cell, so the selection grows with the scroll. In the alternate buffer there is nothing to scroll. In program mode the same zone and speed send wheel steps up (SGR button 64) or down (65) at the cursor instead, so the program scrolls its own view (and extends its selection, if it does that); the page view does not move. Speed and the carried fraction: `scrollStep`.
- **Lift.** In program mode the lift first marks a gesture for the terminal's clipboard (`mouseBallTarget().gesture`, `handle.gesture()` of `terminal-clipboard.js`) and then sends the release at the cursor; the ball copies nothing itself. The program's copy that follows within `GESTURE_WINDOW_MS` (1500 ms) is written at once with the notice "Copied N characters" instead of the "Copy request from the terminal" bar; when the browser refuses that write (the program's copy arrives after the user activation of the lift, as on iOS Safari) the refusal bar finishes it. In page mode, if the button was down, it goes up and the selection is copied through the copy path of the double tap (`terminal-clipboard.js` `handle.copy(text)`, called inside the `pointerup`, so the browser still counts the user activation): the notice "Copied N characters", the refusal bar `#h5-clipboard-bar` ("Press Copy to finish copying") when the browser says no, the duplicate merge. The highlight stays until the next tap or new output. Without a press, lifting only hides the cursor: a plain drag positions, nothing is clicked. A cancelled touch (`pointercancel`, `lostpointercapture`) ends everything: nothing is copied and the selection is cleared; in program mode the program still gets the release (it must not be left with its button down, and xterm drops its document listeners on it), without a gesture mark, so a copy it makes then waits on the request bar. The same release goes out when the terminal goes away during the touch.
- **Copied text** (page mode; v2.174.1: "copying a wrapped line turns into a newline"). The text is not `term.getSelection()` but the rows of the selection read from the buffer and joined by `joinSelection` in `mouse-ball-model.mjs`. A row xterm soft-wrapped continues the one before it as it is. Claude Code and Codex cut their own lines at the terminal width with real line breaks, which xterm cannot know: a row after a row that runs to the right edge (within `HARD_WRAP_SLACK` = 2 columns, a long word or path cut in the middle: joined with nothing, or with one space when the row has words) or after a row whose next word would not have fitted (a break at a space: joined with one space) is the rest of the same line. A row that starts a list item, a quote, a heading or a table row, a blank row, and a row after a short row stay new lines; a selection that stops inside a row does not join it. The same rule is not applied to the double and triple tap copies.
- **Back into the handle.** On the lift, a cancel or the end of the touch any other way, the ball glides back into the handle: 220 ms (`GLIDE_MS`, ease-in) to the middle of the bar (`glideTarget`, inside the visible terminal), shrinking to its 11px width and fading out; `data-state="glide"`, the handle `data-mouse="glide"`. Then the ball is hidden, `data-state="idle"` and the handle's `data-mouse` goes. With `prefers-reduced-motion` there is no glide. A new touch during the glide ends it at once (a capture `pointerdown` listener) and is an ordinary handle touch.
- **The terminal underneath.** While the ball is out the terminal neither scrolls, pans nor double-tap-selects: the finger is on the handle, outside `.h5-xterm` (so the terminal's listeners, `terminal-gestures.js` included, never see the touch), the handle keeps the pointer capture and has `touch-action: none`, no text selection or callout, and the chunk cancels `touchmove` and `contextmenu` on the document while the ball is out (non-passive listeners, added only then). One finger drives; a second finger does not move the ball. A terminal that goes away during the touch (another session, the chat) ends it, as do a hidden page, `pagehide` and window `blur`.
- **First-time hint.** The first time the handle can be used on a device (a terminal shown with the handle, the handle not touched or moved, the ball not out), a pill shows above the mouse handle, toward the inside (24px in from the terminal's edge), for 4 s: "Drag to scroll, slide left for the mouse" with the library `left` chevron (`chevronLeft`, primary colour), or, with the handle on the left edge, "Drag to scroll, slide right for the mouse" with the library `right` chevron after the text (`treeChevron`; before the handle scrolled it was "Slide the mouse handle left", and before 2026-11-27 "Slide the scrollbar left for the mouse") (MUI `Paper` elevation 6, 44px high on one line (it fits beside the chevron on a 390px phone; the longer "Drag the handle to scroll, slide it left for the mouse" did not), wrapping to two lines on a narrower screen and then placed by its real height, a 22px radius, body2, `role="status"`, `pointer-events: none`). The same text with a mouse. Any touch or press hides it at once. Once per device: localStorage `h5-mouse-ball-hinted` = `shown` (category hardware, through `local-keys.mjs`, not per pane), written when it shows.
- **The mouse handle** (2026-11-27; the user: "a separate mouse handle on the right edge ... only for the mouse ... this replaces the real mouse"; mockup confirmed: idle, slide the handle left, long press to move, snapped to the left edge; later the same day, mockup confirmed: "Drag the handle up or down: scroll", and the wheel strip removed). The terminal's one tool besides its body: it scrolls, the ball comes out of it and it can be moved. Drawn by the ball's chunk (`MouseBall.jsx`, `#h5-mouse-handle`; nothing in the first screen), whenever a terminal is shown (`mouseBallTarget()` gives one; hidden otherwise), on the phone and the computer alike, in both input modes, the same size. Its first 10px of travel decide what a drag is (`handleAxis(dx, dy, side)`): at least as much up or down as sideways is a scroll; more sideways toward the inside is the ball; more sideways toward the edge is nothing. Held still (under 6px) for 500 ms it is moved. The first answer is kept until the lift: a drag that starts vertical and turns sideways stays a scroll, and a scroll or a pull-out never becomes a move (the move's timer is stopped by the first 6px of travel, before the 10px decision). A tap (no travel) does nothing: its `click` is cancelled on the handle and never reaches the terminal or the page.
  - **Look.** A bar: 11 x 64px, round ends (radius 6.5), white 55% with a 1px white 35% edge, a 7 x 9px corner-cursor glyph inside (`M1 8 L1 1 L6 1`, white, 1.5px). `role="img"`, `aria-label` "Mouse handle, drag to scroll" (it stays an image, not a `button` or a `slider`: it has no keyboard action, and keyboard users scroll with the keys; the name says what a finger or a mouse does with it). Its touch box is 44 x 72px (at least 44px in touch input), the bar inside it at the edge, 2px in (the browser's edge swipe stays free). `position: fixed`, drawn inside `#h5-app` (a portal, so it layers with the page's own parts and is inert with it while the phone's task drawer is open) at z-index 2: over the terminal, under the bottom bar (3, whose saved commands menu and draft box reach up over the terminal), the copy bar (3) and everything MUI layers (menus, dialogs, drawers, notices). Its area ends at the top of the bottom bar where that bar grows up over the terminal's box (the draft box), and it is placed again when either box changes size (`ResizeObserver`); `touch-action: none`, no text selection, no callout, no context menu, a mouse press keeps the keyboard where it is. A mouse wheel or trackpad over it goes to the terminal under it at the same point (a pinch stays the browser's), so the wheel works over the whole terminal as before.
  - **Place.** On the right edge by default, the bar's centre 68px above the bottom of the visible terminal (`#h5-terminal` within the visual viewport, so it follows the on-screen keyboard), i.e. the bar ends 36px above it, clear of the key row: on the design's 844px phone the bar runs from 640 to 704. The touch box always stays inside the terminal (`handleCentre`). It is placed again on every store change, resize, visual-viewport change and every 500 ms.
  - **Scrolling** (the old wheel strip's spring, moved to the handle; mockup "Drag the handle up or down: scroll"). A vertical drag pulls the handle up or down from its place: the bar follows the finger (kept inside the visible terminal, so from its low default place it soon stops at the bottom while the finger goes on), pulled up scrolls to older lines, pulled down to newer, the same in the normal and the alternate buffer. The pull is the finger's travel from the touch, at most 42% of the visible terminal's height (`springPull`, `SPRING.reach`); the speed is 8 lines per second per terminal row of pull beyond a 6px dead zone, at most 200 lines per second (`springSpeed`, `SPRING` in `mouse-ball-model.mjs`, the strip's constants), counted by real time (`performance.now()`, every frame, every move, every 250 ms check and on the lift, through `scrollStep`), so slow frames do not slow it. The steps: in the normal buffer `term.scrollLines`; in the alternate buffer (Claude Code, Codex, vim, tmux, opencode) wheel events on `.xterm-screen` (one per line, up to 200 per step), which xterm forwards as the program's mouse wheel reports or, without mouse mode, as up / down arrows, pointed at the middle of the visible terminal (`.h5-xterm`), not at the handle: a program that scrolls the pane under the mouse (opencode, "Mouse wheel over a program" in [page-terminal-and-bottom-bar.md](page-terminal-and-bottom-bar.md)) has its main content there. The buffer type is read at every step, so a program that switches screens during a pull is followed. While it scrolls the bar is blue (primary 90%, `data-state="scroll"`) with the self-drawn `chevronUp` and `chevronDown` (14px) 6px above and below it, the one of the pull's direction in the primary colour, the other white 40% (`data-dir` `up` / `down`); no position line and no track (the mockup's caption pill is not built). On the lift the scrolling stops and the handle springs back to its place in 200 ms (`SPRING_MS`, the strip's small overshoot `cubic-bezier(.34, 1.56, .64, 1)`, `data-state="spring"`, then `idle`; at once with reduced motion); no momentum. The same with the left mouse button. It never gets stuck when the browser swallows the end of a touch (the strip's rules, `scripts/test-h5-wheel-stuck-browser.cjs`): a new touch on the handle always takes over from one still open (a second finger too: the first one then does nothing); the scroll ends as if cancelled on `pointercancel`, `lostpointercapture`, a hidden page, `pagehide`, window `blur` and a switched or closed terminal (checked every 250 ms and at every move), and when no pointer event came for 3 s (`STALE_MS`) the scrolling stops and the handle goes back (a held, perfectly still finger stops after 3 s; its next move brings both back). Test hook: `window.__h5HandleScrolling()` (a scroll frame is pending). The terminal body keeps its own touch scrolling ("Touch scrolling" in [page-terminal-and-bottom-bar.md](page-terminal-and-bottom-bar.md)); with the strip gone, nothing on the body depended on it.
  - **Pull out the ball.** A slide toward the inside of the screen (to the left on the right edge, to the right on the left edge), decided in the first 10px (`handleAxis`), hands the finger to the ball (`begin({ id, from, at, side })`): under 40px the handle stretches toward the finger (the same head and tail, mirrored on the left edge, from the handle's 11px width: `stretchProgress` / `stretchShape` with the side), the bar blue (primary 90%) while it is pulled (`data-mouse="stretch"`); lifted then, it springs back and nothing happens. At 40px it is the same 52px ball with the same cursor, dwell, press, page selection or program mode, scroll, copy, Escape and status pill. The ball glides back to the handle's bar (the middle of the bar, shrinking to its 11px width). Toward the edge, or a short drag: nothing happens (no scroll, no ball, no move); up or down scrolls (above).
  - **Long press to move.** A finger (or the left mouse button) held on the handle moving less than 6px (`HOLD_SLOP`, as `DWELL_SLOP`) for 500 ms (`HOLD_MS`) picks it up: first the haptic tick (`keyTick()`, before anything that could move the focus: on iOS the tick toggles a hidden switch), then the bar grows to 18 x 84px, turns blue (primary 90%) with a shadow (0 6px 16px black 70%) and follows the finger in 2D from where it was held (no jump), its bar kept inside the visible terminal (below the header, above the key row; `clampCentre`). Meanwhile two dashed lines (2px, primary 70%) mark the terminal's left and right edges (`#h5-mouse-handle-guides`) and a pill (`#h5-mouse-handle-pill`, `role="status"`, 60px in from both edges at 35% of the height) says "Let go: it snaps to the nearest edge". On the lift it glides 160 ms (`SNAP_MS`, ease-out; at once with reduced motion) to the nearer of the two edges (`nearestEdge`; exactly the middle goes right) at the height it was let go, kept inside (`snapPlace`), and back to 11 x 64. A slide of more than 6px before the 500 ms is never a move. Escape (a mouse, or a keyboard beside the finger) and a cancelled touch, a hidden page, `pagehide` or window `blur` put it back where it was, and nothing is kept.
  - **Kept per device.** localStorage `h5-mouse-handle` (category hardware in `public/js/local-keys.js`: a place for the finger on this screen, not synced; through `local-keys.mjs`), JSON `{"edge":"right","y":0.8}`: the edge and the bar's centre as a fraction (0 to 1, three decimals) of the visible terminal's height, written at each snap. Absent: the default place. Anything else (not JSON, an unknown edge) is the default; a bad height alone keeps the edge with the default height (`parsePlace`).
  - **Left edge.** Everything mirrored: the slide to the right pulls the ball out, the stretch's head is never left of the handle. The cursor stays 46px up-left of the ball and is kept inside the visible cells (`cursorFor`), so it reaches the first column (the ball at 46px from the left) and, with the edge fade of the right edge, the last column, from either side.
  - **Edge cases.** The copy bar and the saved commands menu lie over a handle under them. With the terminal smaller than the touch box the handle sits in its middle. Its touch box (44 x 72) covers a few columns at the edge: a touch there is the handle's, so a link or a cell under it is reached by moving the handle (a long press) or by scrolling the text from the body.
- **Accessibility.** The handle is `role="img"` with the name "Mouse handle, drag to scroll" (Chinese in `i18n-zh-ui.js`); the old strip's `role="scrollbar"`, its name "Scroll" and its description "Slide left for the mouse" went with it. The ball and the cursor are `aria-hidden`: they are a touch tool, and keyboard users scroll with the keys and select with the keyboard and the copy view.
- **Limits** (not in this version): the mouse handle, its scrolling included, was not tried on a real phone or with a real mouse (headless Chromium with CDP touches, in-page pointer events and Playwright's mouse only; the feel of the spring's speed and of the 44 x 72 touch box for scrolling is not known), nor the haptic tick of its long press on iOS; whether the hover and the focus make Claude Code's fullscreen selection work is not known yet (not tried with a real Claude Code session or a real phone); only the left button; no modifiers (Shift or Option to force the page selection over a program, as a desktop mouse can). A program that reports the mouse but does not select or copy on its own gets the clicks and drags and nothing is copied; turn its mouse mode off (or use the copy view) to copy in the page. A click without selection (a tap at the cursor) does nothing. No horizontal scrolling while selecting in Wide mode (the cursor is kept inside the visible cells). The copy on lift relies on `pointerup` counting as a user activation on iOS Safari; where it does not, the refusal bar finishes the copy. Not tried on a real phone (only headless Chromium with CDP touches) and not with a real Claude Code fullscreen session (the test plays the program with SGR drag tracking and an OSC 52 reply).
